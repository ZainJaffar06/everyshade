Spots you are watching

What would make a spot worth a doctor visit

  • It is new, or you do not remember it being there.
  • It has changed in size, shape, color, or how it feels since last month.
  • It bleeds, itches, hurts, crusts, or does not heal in about three weeks.
  • It looks different from all your other spots.
  • It is a new dark line in a nail, or a dark line that is getting wider.
  • You are worried about it. That is reason enough to ask.

This tool cannot tell you whether a spot is harmful. Only a doctor who examines you can.

    Every Shade · Monthly skin check

    Once a month, in good light, with a full-length mirror and a hand mirror. Ask someone to help with your back and scalp. Tick each area as you go.

    Name: ______________________

    Date: ______________

    Areas to check

    • Face, ears, and neck
    • Scalp (part the hair in rows)
    • Chest, belly, and skin folds
    • Arms, armpits, tops of hands
    • Palms and between fingers
    • Fingernails
    • Back, shoulders, buttocks
    • Legs, front and back, groin
    • Soles and between toes
    • Toenails

    Bold = often missed on brown and Black skin.

    Look for

    • • A spot that is new, or that you do not remember.
    • • A spot that changed in size, shape, or color.
    • • A sore that bleeds, crusts, or does not heal in about 3 weeks.
    • • A spot that looks different from all the others.
    • • A shiny bump, whether pink, brown, or black.
    • • A new dark line in a nail, especially one that widens, bleeds, or darkens the skin beside the nail.

    ABCDE of melanoma

    Asymmetry · Border uneven · Color mixed · Diameter over a pencil eraser · Evolving (changing)

    If you find something

    See a dermatologist or your primary care doctor about any spot that is new, changing, bleeding, not healing, or a new dark line in a nail. Medicare covers a visit to check a spot when it is medically necessary. NJ community health centers see patients regardless of insurance: findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov. Free AAD SPOTme screenings: aad.org/public/public-health/skin-cancer-screenings
