About us

Skin cancer education that looks like New Jersey.

Every Shade is a student-run group at Rutgers University–New Brunswick. We give free talks to older adults on how skin cancer looks on every skin tone, and we help people find free or low-cost care.

Our mission

Help every New Jerseyan, whatever their skin tone or language, recognize the early signs of skin cancer, check their own skin once a month, and know where to go when a spot worries them.

The problem we work on

Melanoma is found later, on average, in people with darker skin. Five-year survival is 66% for Black patients and 90% for white patients (Culp and Lunsford, CDC, 2019). Most teaching pictures show light skin: about 15% of images in common medical study resources show skin of color (Perlman et al., 2021). And on darker skin, melanoma often appears where people do not look: the palms, the soles of the feet, and under the nails.

How we work

Our standards

  • We teach; we never diagnose. Our members are students. If someone shows us a spot, we help them find a clinician.
  • Physician-reviewed content. Every medical statement comes from the American Academy of Dermatology, the Skin Cancer Foundation, the CDC, or peer-reviewed research, and the talk and handout are reviewed by a physician before use and again each year.
  • Native-speaker review. No translated material is handed out until a native speaker has checked it.
  • Measured, and published. Every talk is measured with the same anonymous quiz, and we post the results on our Impact page, good or bad.
  • Privacy first. Quizzes are anonymous, we never photograph anyone's skin, and our self-check tool stores nothing on any server. See our privacy page.
  • No product endorsements. We do not promote any brand, product, or clinic in our talks.

Who we are

Every Shade was co-founded in 2026 by Zain Jaffar, an Honors College student at Rutgers University–New Brunswick (Cell Biology and Neuroscience, Data Science minor) who works as a dermatology and Mohs surgery medical assistant and does melanoma and teledermatology research. Student speakers complete a training and certification program before giving talks.
