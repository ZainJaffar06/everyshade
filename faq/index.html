Questions

Frequently asked questions

Something not answered here? Email everyshadescreening@gmail.com or call (915) 400-7072.

Is the talk really free?

Yes. Talks, handouts, and the self-check tool are free. We never sell anything or collect payment.

Who gives the talks?

Trained Rutgers students. We say at the start of every talk that we are students, not doctors. Our content is reviewed by a physician.

Will you look at my spot?

No. Students cannot examine or diagnose skin. If you are worried about a spot, we will help you find a clinician, including free and sliding-fee clinics on our care list.

How long is a talk, and what do you need from us?

About 45 minutes including questions. For in-person talks we need a room and a screen or wall to project on; for Zoom, a TV or projector in the room, or a link sent to members. We bring or email the large-print handouts.

Can the talk be in another language?

Handouts are ready in Spanish, Hindi, Urdu, Gujarati, Haitian Creole, and Korean. For a talk in one of these languages, tell us when you book and we will pair our speaker with a student who speaks it.

What is the quiz, and is it anonymous?

A short multiple-choice quiz before and after the talk, so we can see whether the talk works. No names, no health questions. Results are only reported as group averages.

Is the self-check tool safe to use?

Yes. Photos and notes stay on your own phone or computer. There are no accounts and nothing is uploaded. It is a guide, not a diagnosis.

Who can book a talk?

Any New Jersey senior center, library, faith community, senior housing building, community center, civic club, or cultural association.

I am a student. How do I join?

See Get involved. Speakers complete training and a practice run before giving a talk.

Who pays for this?

Every Shade is volunteer-run. Our costs are mostly printing. If your organization would like to support printing or donate sunscreen for attendees, email us.

Every Shade provides general health education only. Nothing on this site or in our talks is medical advice or a diagnosis. Our members are students, not medical professionals. If a spot is new, changing, or bleeding, please see a doctor.
