# Reviewer guide: Every Shade skin-check handout translations

Thank you for checking this. The handout is a 2-page, large-print sheet given
to older adults at community talks. It teaches people how to look at their own
skin once a month and when to see a doctor. It is **education, not diagnosis**,
and the people handing it out are students, not clinicians.

These translations were drafted by machine. Please read your language's PDF
side by side with `../talk-kit/handout/handout-en.pdf` and mark anything that
should change. Edits can go straight into the `.html` file, or on a printed copy.
When you are satisfied, sign the "Translation reviewed by" line at the bottom of
page 2 (name and date).

## Checklist

**Meaning and tone**
- [ ] Nothing is lost, added, or softened compared with the English. In
      particular, the "See a doctor about a spot that is..." box and the
      "spots people miss" box must keep their full meaning.
- [ ] Reads like a community health worker talking, not a textbook. Target is
      roughly a 6th-grade reading level: short sentences, everyday words.
- [ ] Register is warm and respectful for older adults (polite "you" form where
      the language has one).
- [ ] Nothing reads as a diagnosis or a promise ("this spot is cancer",
      "you will be cured"). The English says "most are curable" and "see a
      doctor"; keep it at that level.
- [ ] Women-specific line (step 3) and the body-part names are plain and not
      embarrassing in your community's usage.

**Medical terms**
- [ ] Each medical term uses the everyday word first, with the clinical term in
      parentheses the first time only where that helps (see the table below).
- [ ] "Melanoma" is recognizable to a patient who later hears it from a doctor.
- [ ] "ABCDE" keeps the English letters A B C D E; only the words after them are
      translated. A reader should be able to connect each letter to its word.
- [ ] Color words (brown, black, pink, purple, tan, pearly, shiny) match how
      people in your community actually describe skin and spots.

**Layout and print**
- [ ] Exactly 2 pages, nothing cut off at the bottom of either page.
- [ ] No empty boxes, question marks, or wrong-looking letters (missing glyphs).
- [ ] Text is large enough for an older reader. If a wording change makes it
      spill to a third page, tell us; we will shorten elsewhere rather than
      shrink the type much further.
- [ ] Numbers, URLs, the email, and the phone number are unchanged and readable.
- [ ] **Urdu only:** the page reads right-to-left correctly; step numbers 1-8
      sit on the right; URLs and the email read left-to-right inside the Urdu
      text; no letters are broken apart where they should join.
- [ ] **Hindi / Gujarati only:** vowel signs and conjuncts render correctly
      (no detached matras, no stacked-wrong letters).
- [ ] **Korean only:** line breaks fall between words, not mid-word.

**Things that must stay in English**
- "every shade" / "Every Shade", all URLs, the email address, the phone number,
  the "Sources:" line, "Medicare", "American Academy of Dermatology".

## Other wording we were least sure about

- **Spanish:** "café" vs "marrón" for brown (regional); "bultito" for bump;
  "el patito feo"; "letra grande" for large print.
- **Hindi:** "दाग़" for spot (vs "धब्बा"/"निशान"); "पपड़ी" for crust and scale;
  "सबसे अलग दिखने वाला" for "ugly duckling"; "रोग-प्रतिरोधक क्षमता" may be
  too formal for immune system.
- **Urdu:** "دھبہ" for spot; "کھرنڈ" for crust/scaly; "قوتِ مدافعت" for immune
  system; "سب سے الگ نظر آنے والا" for "ugly duckling". Western digits (10, 50,
  3 to 4) are used to match the phone number and URLs; say if Urdu digits are
  preferred.
- **Gujarati:** "ડાઘ" for spot; "પોપડી" for crust/scaly; "ચાંદું" for sore;
  "બહેનો" for "Women" in step 3.
- **Haitian Creole:** "tach" for spot; "kal" for scaly; "kabann bwonzaj" for
  tanning bed; "ti kanna lèd" for "ugly duckling"; "gren bote" for mole.
- **Korean:** "점" vs "반점" for spot; "지역 보건센터" for community health
  center; "소득에 따라 비용을 낮춰 줍니다" for sliding fee; honorific level
  (-세요 / -습니다) consistent and appropriate for elders.
