# Every Shade speaker training and certification

Every talk must sound the same no matter who gives it: accurate, calm, never diagnostic. This program takes a new volunteer from zero to certified speaker in about four hours over two weeks.

## The path

| Step | What the volunteer does | Time | Signed off by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Read | The deck with speaker notes, the handout, the medical review packet, and the "Running the talk" section of the chapter toolkit | 60 min | Self |
| 2. Watch | One live talk (or a recording, if the host agreed to recording) | 45 min | Self |
| 3. Knowledge check | The 15-question speaker check below; pass mark 13 of 15, with questions 11 to 15 all correct | 15 min | Speaker Director |
| 4. Practice run | Give the full talk to two Every Shade members acting as an audience, including a 10-minute mock Q&A with the hard questions below | 60 min | Speaker Director plus one certified speaker |
| 5. Co-present | Give half of a real talk alongside a certified speaker | 45 min | The certified speaker |
| 6. Certified | Added to the speaker roster; may lead talks | | President |

Language-team speakers do steps 1 to 6 in their language, with the practice run observed by a native speaker of that language where possible.

## The five rules (memorize these)

1. **We teach; we never diagnose.** Not even "that looks fine."
2. **We say who we are.** "We are students, not doctors" at the start of every talk.
3. **We stay on the slides for medical content.** If a question goes beyond them, say so and point to a doctor.
4. **We always end with where to get care,** including free and sliding-fee options.
5. **We protect privacy.** Quizzes are anonymous; we never take photos of attendees' skin or collect health details.

## Speaker knowledge check (15 questions)

1. Where is melanoma most often missed on Black and brown skin? *(Palms, soles, between the toes, under the nails.)*
2. Name the five letters of ABCDE and what each means. *(Asymmetry, Border, Color, Diameter, Evolving.)*
3. Which letter matters most, and why? *(E: any change is a reason to see a doctor.)*
4. What is the "ugly duckling" sign? *(A spot that looks different from all the others.)*
5. How can basal cell carcinoma look on darker skin? *(Often brown or black and shiny, not pink.)*
6. Where does squamous cell carcinoma often start on darker skin? *(In old scars, burns, or long-lasting sores.)*
7. When is a dark nail line worth a doctor visit? *(New, widening, darker, uneven, spreading to the skin around the nail, or the nail splitting/bleeding; a new single line after 50.)*
8. List four risk factors. *(Past sunburns or tanning beds, age over 50, family history of melanoma, many moles or prior skin cancer, weak immune system, chronic scar or sore.)*
9. What are the six "see a doctor" signs on slide 15? *(New, changing, bleeding, not healing in 3 to 4 weeks, dark nail line, different.)*
10. Where can someone without insurance go? *(A community health center on a sliding fee: findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov or 1-877-464-4772; our care finder page.)*
11. An attendee holds a spot up to the camera and asks, "Is this cancer?" What do you say? *(Use the redirect script below; no opinion on the spot.)*
12. An attendee says a spot has bled on and off for two months. What do you do? *(Encourage a doctor visit this month; offer to help find a clinic; do not reassure.)*
13. Someone asks if they should stop a medication or try a home remedy. *(We can't advise on treatment; please ask your doctor or pharmacist.)*
14. A host asks if Every Shade can do skin checks at the next event. *(Only with a licensed clinical partner; students never examine skin.)*
15. Someone becomes upset or frightened during the talk. *(Acknowledge, reassure that most spots are harmless, emphasize that checking early is the point, offer help finding care, follow up privately after.)*

## The redirect script (say it word for word)

> "I can't tell you what that is, and I'd never want to guess wrong. It's exactly the kind of spot worth showing a doctor. If you don't have one, stay on after the talk, or email us, and we'll help you find a free or low-cost place nearby."

## Hard questions for the mock Q&A

- "My doctor said my mole is fine. Should I get a second opinion?" *(If it changes, go back; you can always ask for a dermatology referral.)*
- "Does sunscreen cause cancer?" *(No; the AAD recommends SPF 30+ for everyone. Point to the AAD sunscreen FAQ.)*
- "I'm Black. Do I really need sunscreen?" *(Melanin helps but does not fully protect; and acral melanoma is not caused by sun, so checking matters most.)*
- "How much does a skin check cost?" *(Depends on insurance; Medicare covers a visit for a spot that worries you when medically necessary; sliding-fee clinics charge by income.)*
- "Can I use an app to check my moles?" *(Our self-check tool helps you look and track changes; no app should replace a doctor.)*
- "Are you going to be doctors?" *(Many of us hope to; today we are students teaching what to look for.)*

## Delivery standards

- Pace: about 120 words a minute; pause after each slide's key line.
- Captions on for Zoom; large text; no jargon without a plain-word explanation.
- Launch the before quiz at slide 2 and the after quiz at slide 19; give 3 minutes each.
- After every talk: enter quiz results within 7 days; log attendance and questions asked in the outreach tracker; send the host a thank-you note within 48 hours.
