# Clinical photo sources for the Every Shade senior talk

Researched 2026-11-10 for slides 7, 8, 10 and 11 of `slides/Every-Shade-Senior-Talk-2026-11-20.pptx`.
No images were downloaded during research. Every candidate is a clinical photo (not dermoscopy-only, not histology) from an authoritative source, and its diagnosis label comes from that source.

Skin tone column: "stated" means the source gives it. "Est." is my visual estimate from the photo, not a clinical assessment.

Two facts that changed since the original plan:

- **NCI Visuals Online is offline.** `visualsonline.cancer.gov` returns NXDOMAIN from Google and Cloudflare DNS as of today. The last Wayback snapshots are from early 2026. The NCI images are still US-government public domain. Get the files from their Wikimedia Commons mirrors, below, and use the Wayback snapshot as the record of NCI's own label and reuse statement.
- **DermNet's licence is now CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, not 3.0 NZ. From https://dermnetnz.org/image-licence: "You may use Watermarked Images in accordance with the licensing requirements of Attribution-NonCommercial-NoDerivatives 4.0." It requires credit to DermNet with a link to the specific image page, for example "Image sourced from DermNet". The watermark must stay. Print runs over 500 copies count as commercial. The free images are 640x480 and watermarked.

## Full Spectrum of Dermatology atlas: Image Use Policy, verified

Policy page: https://jddonline.com/project-atlas/image-use-policy/ (FAQ: https://jddonline.com/project-atlas/derm-atlas-faqs)

- **Scope (quoted):** "You may use only the watermarked images, unless given written permission otherwise, for printed and/or electronic non-commercial, clinical and/or educational use. This includes consulting, lectures, and other teaching materials presented within an academic institution, academic setting, and/or clinic."
- **No changes:** the watermark must stay intact, and "The image is not to be cropped, altered, or modified ... Images must be used as provided."
- **Outside scope:** images may not be reproduced, republished, posted, transmitted or distributed outside those terms without written permission.
- **Credit line:** on the same slide as the image, with the title in italics: *Image source: The Full Spectrum of Dermatology: A Diverse and Inclusive Atlas*. (The FAQ phrases it as "Image Credit: ...", but the policy's "Image source:" wording governs.)
- **Resolution:** logged-out downloads are 300x300. Higher resolution needs a JDD login ("Log into your JDD account to access high resolution images and request permissions").

**Plain verdict:** the atlas allows non-commercial educational lectures, and that covers slides in a lecture. However, the policy names only lectures "within an academic institution, academic setting, and/or clinic". A Rutgers student group giving a free public Zoom talk to community seniors is plausibly an "academic setting", but the policy does not name it. Three activities go further and would need written permission:

- recording the talk and posting it
- posting the deck publicly, including committing it to the public `ZainJaffar06/everyshade` GitHub repo
- distributing the deck as a handout

If you use an atlas image, send a one-line permission request through the atlas contact page first. The recommended picks above avoid the atlas, so you do not need permission for them.

## How to use these legally

- **Credit every photo on the slide itself**, in small text under the image, using the exact line in the table. A final "Photo credits" slide is fine as an addition, but the atlas requires credit on the same page, so on-slide credit is the safe default for everything. For DermNet, the link to the specific image page is required. Make it a clickable hyperlink in the PPTX and in any PDF handout, and also show the URL as text so Zoom viewers can see it.
- **ND (No Derivatives: DermNet) means no cropping, no editing, no colour changes, and no removing the watermark.** Resize proportionally only. If the image doesn't fit the placeholder, change the placeholder to fit the image, not the other way round. The Full Spectrum atlas has the same no-cropping rule.
- **NC (Non-Commercial: DermNet, Annals of Dermatology, and the atlas by policy)** means no selling, no sponsors, and no ads. A free talk by a student group fits. DermNet treats print runs over 500 copies as commercial, so keep printed handouts with DermNet images under 500.
- **CC BY (Cureus, Medicine, Pan African Medical Journal) and public domain (NCI, CDC)** allow cropping and editing. For CC BY, add "(cropped)" or "(arrow removed)" to the credit if you change the image, and keep the licence link. Public-domain images need no credit by law, but NCI and CDC ask for it, so include it.
- **Do not use the unwatermarked or higher-resolution files** of DermNet or atlas images unless you have written permission. Use the free watermarked versions linked above.
- **Patient privacy:** none of the recommended picks shows an identifiable face. Do not add patient details beyond the published captions.

## Not found / caveats

- **No darker-skin nail photo with a caption that explicitly states Hutchinson's sign.** The darker-skin pick (3A) shows a wide, variegated band, and the photos with Hutchinson's sign stated (3B, 3C) are on Fitzpatrick III skin. Nail melanoma with Hutchinson's sign in a Black patient was not found in any open-access, clearly licensed source.
- **The Full Spectrum atlas has no melanonychia or nail-melanoma images.** Its Nails section covers only lichen planus, onychomycosis, onychoschizia, paronychia, psoriasis, retronychia and mucous cyst.
- **NCI Visuals Online is offline**, so NCI pages are cited through Wayback snapshots and the files through Wikimedia Commons. No NCI or CDC image of ALM on darker skin was found. The NCI foot melanoma, #9246 "Skin Cancer, Melanoma, Foot" (https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Melanoma_(1).jpg), is on light skin.
- **Rejected:** the Wikimedia Commons "Longitudinal melanonychia.png" (its caption does not state melanoma), the Commons "Photography of a large acral lentiginous melanoma" from *Diagnostic Pathology* 2015 (CC BY 4.0, but light skin), and Commons user uploads labelled "Own work", whose diagnosis cannot be verified.
- DermNet's free images are 640x480. That is enough for a Zoom screen share at roughly half-slide size, but they will look soft full-screen.
