# 03. Roles and the minimum viable team

Five roles, three people minimum. One person can hold two roles; nobody holds three. Time estimates assume two to four talks a month during the semester.

## President (2 to 3 hours a week)

Owns the relationship with the advisor and with Every Shade (Rutgers). Runs the weekly 30-minute meeting. Keeps the student organization registration current. Signs the chapter agreement. Makes sure the twice-yearly report goes out and that the chapter email is answered within 48 hours.

The week: 30-minute meeting, 30 minutes of email, 30 minutes checking the tracker and data folder, an hour on whatever is falling behind. In a three-person chapter the president is also the speaker lead or the outreach lead.

What goes wrong: the president does everything, burns out by week 10, and the chapter stops. Give away roles in week 1 and hold people to them.

## Outreach lead (3 to 4 hours a week)

Owns the target list and the tracker. Sends the emails and follow-ups, makes the calls, confirms logistics one week and one day before each talk. This role produces talks; without it nothing else matters.

The week: an hour building or refreshing the list, an hour on first emails and day-5 follow-ups, an hour on the phone, 30 minutes updating the tracker. Double it during a booking push.

Who is good at this: someone unafraid of the phone who writes short emails and keeps a tidy list. The outreach lead often never presents.

What goes wrong: emails go out, nobody follows up, and the chapter concludes "senior centers aren't interested." They are interested. They did not get a call.

## Speaker lead (2 to 3 hours a week, plus talks)

Owns the talk. Knows the deck cold, trains every other presenter, and is the default presenter. Runs the Zoom setup (section 05). Keeps the kit synced with the latest version in the Rutgers repo. Makes sure the disclaimer slide and the Q&A rules are never skipped.

Each talk costs about 2.5 hours: 30 minutes of prep and tech check, 45 minutes of talk, 15 minutes of Q&A and packing up, plus travel. Training a new presenter costs about three hours across two sessions.

What goes wrong: a presenter improvises a medical claim or answers a "look at my spot" question. Prevent it by making every presenter practice the redirect script aloud before their first talk.

## Measurement lead (1 to 2 hours a week)

Owns the pre/post quiz, the attendance log, the 30-day follow-up, and the report. Prints quiz sheets, sets up Zoom polls, collects sheets, enters numbers within 48 hours, computes knowledge gain (section 06), sends follow-ups, and writes the semester report with the president.

Who is good at this: someone who likes spreadsheets and is comfortable saying "that number is lower than we hoped."

## Language lead (1 to 2 hours a week, more when translating)

Owns the translated handouts in languages/, knows which have been reviewed by a native speaker with a medical background, recruits reviewers (section 08), and decides when a talk needs an interpreter. Also keeps the local free and low-cost clinic list current, because it changes and often needs to exist in more than one language.

If nobody on your team speaks a second language, you still need this role. The job is to find reviewers, not to translate.

## Minimum viable team

| Person | Roles |
|---|---|
| A | President and speaker lead |
| B | Outreach lead |
| C | Measurement lead and language lead |

About 7 to 9 hours a week across three people. With four, split speaker lead from president. With five, everyone holds one role and four talks a month is comfortable.

## Second presenters

Keep at least two trained presenters so a talk is never cancelled for one person's exam. A trained presenter has given the full talk to a real audience with the speaker lead watching. Keep the list in the Admin folder.

## Advisor (about 3 hours a year)

Reads the deck once a year, suggests edits, replies to the annual sign-off email, and agrees to be named. Send one email in September with the deck, one in January with the report, and a thank-you in May. Do not send the advisor your tracker, social posts, or meeting notes.

## Weekly meeting agenda (30 minutes)

1. Talks in the next two weeks: presenter, tech, quizzes. (5 min)
2. Tracker: emails out, follow-ups due, calls due, talks booked. (10 min)
3. Last talk's numbers: attendance, knowledge gain, anything that went wrong. (5 min)
4. Blockers. (5 min)
5. One thing each person will do before next week. (5 min)

A chapter that meets for two hours a week and gives no talks is a club, not a chapter.
