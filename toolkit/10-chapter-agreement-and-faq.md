# 10. Chapter agreement and FAQ

This is the agreement between Every Shade (Rutgers University–New Brunswick) and a new chapter. It is written in plain English on purpose: a promise between student groups about how a shared name and shared materials will be used, not a contract between corporations. Both sides sign by email. It renews each year with the fall report.

## Chapter agreement

```
EVERY SHADE CHAPTER AGREEMENT

Between: Every Shade, a student organization at Rutgers University–New
Brunswick ("Every Shade"), everyshadescreening@gmail.com
And: Every Shade at [School] ("the Chapter"), represented by
[President] and [second founder]
Effective: [date]. Renewed each year with the fall semester report.

1. Name and materials. The Chapter may use the name "Every Shade at
   [School]", the logo, the talk kit, the handouts and translations,
   the quiz, the flyer template, and this toolkit, free, to give free
   health-education talks in its community. Every Shade keeps
   ownership of the name and materials. The Chapter changes only the
   title slide, the local resources slide, and the flyer's event
   details, and uses the most recent version in the repository.

2. Content standards. The Chapter gives the talk as written. It adds
   no medical claims that are not traceable to the American Academy
   of Dermatology, the Skin Cancer Foundation, the CDC, or a
   peer-reviewed source. It uses the disclaimer wording exactly. It
   has a physician or faculty advisor who reviews the deck each year
   and agrees to be named. It uses only licensed images and never
   scrapes images. It gives out only translations reviewed by a
   qualified native speaker.

3. Never diagnose. Chapter members never diagnose, examine, or give
   individual medical advice, in person, on Zoom, by email, or on
   social media. They follow the redirect script and the
   urgent-symptom procedure in the toolkit.

4. Measurement and reporting. The Chapter runs the pre/post quiz at
   every talk, keeps the attendance log, sends the 30-day follow-up
   where the host allows, and sends Every Shade a semester report by
   January 31 and June 30 using the toolkit template. Every number has
   an n, and no talk is left out.

5. Money. The Chapter does not fundraise, apply for grants, accept
   sponsorship, or sell anything in Every Shade's name without written
   approval from Every Shade. It may accept its own school's student
   organization funding for printing, travel, and interpreters, and
   says so in the report. Nobody is paid. Every Shade charges the
   Chapter nothing and does not fund it.

6. Communications. The Chapter follows the brand and communications
   guide, including the list of claims not to make. Media requests
   beyond the campus paper are shared with Every Shade before the
   Chapter responds.

7. Serving the community. Talks are for the public, with priority for
   senior centers, libraries, faith communities, and organizations
   serving people with brown and Black skin and people with limited
   access to dermatology. Campus talks are fine but do not replace
   community talks.

8. If standards are not met. If Every Shade learns that a Chapter has
   made a diagnosis, added unsupported medical claims, used unlicensed
   images, fundraised in Every Shade's name, or missed two consecutive
   reports, Every Shade emails the Chapter's president and advisor
   describing the issue. The Chapter has 30 days to respond and fix
   it. If it is not fixed, or if the issue is a safety issue (a
   diagnosis, or a claim that could lead someone to delay care), the
   Chapter is paused: it stops giving talks under the Every Shade name
   until the issue is resolved and both sides agree to resume. A
   Chapter paused twice, or one that does not respond within 30 days,
   loses the right to use the name and materials, in writing, and is
   removed from the site.

9. Leaving. The Chapter may end this agreement at any time by email,
   then stops using the name and materials and sends a final report.
   Every Shade may end it under clause 8. Past reports stay in the
   pooled record either way.

10. Changes. Every Shade may update the toolkit, the kit, and this
    agreement, will email every Chapter when it does, and the Chapter
    has one semester to adopt the changes. The Chapter may propose
    changes by email; Every Shade replies within two weeks.

Signed by email:
For Every Shade (Rutgers): [name], [role], [date]
For Every Shade at [School]: [president], [date]; [second founder], [date]
Advisor acknowledged: [name], [title], [date]
```

## FAQ

**Do we have to be pre-med?** No. You have to be willing to learn a 30-slide deck, say "I'm not a doctor" without embarrassment, and call strangers on the phone. Public health, nursing, social work, communications, and language majors are often better at this than pre-meds.

**Can graduate or medical students start a chapter?** Yes, under the same rules, including "never diagnose." A medical student in a white coat gets asked to look at spots more often, not less, so the redirect script matters more.

**Can a high school start a chapter?** Not on its own. A high school group can partner with a nearby college chapter and co-present, with the college chapter responsible for the agreement.

**Do we need our own advisor if the Rutgers advisor already reviewed the deck?** Yes. Your advisor is the person a partner in your area can call, and the one who knows when a local resource has changed. It is the same deck, so the review is a light lift.

**Can we change the talk for our community?** The title slide and the local resources slide. For anything else, send us the proposed change and the source. If it is good, every chapter gets it.

**Can we add a section on sun safety, eczema, hair loss, or acne?** Not inside the Every Shade talk. That is a separate talk under your own student organization's name, with its own review.

**Can we charge a fee or ask for donations?** No. Talks are free and there is no donation ask. Your school's student organization funding can cover printing and travel.

**Can we apply for a grant to cover an interpreter?** Ask us first (clause 5). Usually yes if it is from your school in your student organization's name, and no if it is an outside grant in Every Shade's name.

**What if our school requires a different constitution clause?** Use what your school requires and send us a copy. The agreement above is what matters to us.

**What if a partner wants us to do a screening?** Say no and give them the AAD free skin cancer check finder. If a dermatologist wants to run a screening after your talk, introduce them to the partner and step back.

**What if someone cries, or tells us about a relative who died of melanoma?** Thank them, let the room be quiet for a moment, and continue. Afterward, speak with them one-on-one and listen. No medical opinions.

**What if we only get one talk a semester?** That is a chapter. Report it. One room of 20 people who now check their feet is the program working.

**What if nobody answers our emails?** See section 04. It is almost always "you have not called" or "you are emailing a generic inbox."

**Who owns the data?** The chapter keeps its data sheets; Every Shade keeps the pooled record. Nothing is collected about any individual attendee.

**Can we publish our results?** Talk to your advisor about IRB first, then to us. Anonymous program evaluation data can usually go on a campus poster without IRB review; a journal is different, and we want all chapters' data handled the same way.

**How do we reach you?** everyshadescreening@gmail.com, with "[School] chapter" in the subject line. We reply within a week during the semester.
