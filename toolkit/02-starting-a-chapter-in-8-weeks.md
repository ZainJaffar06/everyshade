# 02. Starting a chapter in 8 weeks

You can go from "I want to do this" to your first booked talk in eight weeks if you do the steps in order. Do not design a logo, build a website, or open an Instagram account until week 6. What gets a talk booked is an advisor, an email address, a practiced speaker, and a list of phone numbers.

## Week 1: Find two or three co-founders

You need at least three people; four is better. Ask two questions: "Who do I know who shows up on time?" and "Who do I know who is comfortable calling a stranger on the phone?" The second is rarer and matters more than any pre-med credential.

Look in your pre-health society, public health and nursing majors, students who already volunteer at a senior center or clinic, and students who speak Spanish, Hindi, Urdu, Gujarati, Haitian Creole, or Korean at home (section 08 explains why). Nobody needs to have taken a dermatology course.

Meet once, in person, for one hour. Assign the roles in section 03, pick a weekly meeting time, and send the chapter application (below) to everyshadescreening@gmail.com by the end of the week.

## Week 2: Find a faculty or physician advisor

Your advisor reads the slide deck once a year and signs off on it, is named as the chapter's medical reviewer, and is the person a community partner can call to confirm the program is real. The advisor does not attend talks or meet with you weekly.

Who to ask, in order: a dermatologist affiliated with your medical school or a nearby practice; any physician on faculty (family medicine, internal medicine, oncology); a nurse practitioner or physician assistant with dermatology experience; a public health faculty member, if a physician will still do the annual content review.

How to ask: a two-paragraph email. First paragraph: who you are, what Every Shade is in one sentence with the site link, and that you will use the Rutgers physician-reviewed deck as-is. Second paragraph: the exact ask, "read a 30-slide deck once a year, suggest edits, and let us list you as our advisor." No attachments. Offer a 15-minute meeting. Follow up once at day 5. If silence at day 10, ask someone else. Ask three people at once if you need to.

Also this week: find out what your school requires to register a student organization. Usually it is an online form, a constitution (we have a template; ask), a minimum member count, and an advisor. Some schools allow a provisional period before you have an advisor. Start the form now.

## Week 3: Register and set up accounts

Submit the registration using the constitution template; change the school name and nothing else unless your school requires a clause.

Create a chapter Gmail account in the form everyshade.[schoolshortname]@gmail.com. Do not use a personal email for outreach. Two people hold the password. Put the disclaimer sentence from section 01 in the signature.

Create a shared Drive folder with four subfolders: Outreach (a copy of outreach/tracker.xlsx from the repo), Talks (the talk-kit/ download), Data (quiz results), and Admin (registration, advisor sign-off).

## Week 4: Learn the talk

Everyone who will present reads the deck, the speaker notes, and the large-print handout, then watches a recorded Rutgers talk (we send it with the kit). Then each presenter gives the talk alone, aloud, with a timer. It should take 32 to 35 minutes without questions. If it takes 45, you are reading the slides.

The speaker lead and the president take the pre/post quiz cold, before reading the deck. That shows you what a smart person who has never heard the talk gets wrong, which is what your audience will get wrong.

## Week 5: Run a practice talk

Give the full talk, with the quiz, to at least eight people who are not in your chapter: your advisor's office staff, a campus staff group, a residence hall floor, your grandparents and their friends on Zoom. Run it exactly as you would at a senior center, including the pre-quiz, the disclaimer, and the Q&A rules. Have one chapter member in the audience ask "Can you look at this spot on my arm?" so the presenter practices the redirect in section 05.

Afterward, compute knowledge gain as in section 06. A question with zero or negative gain means that slide's delivery is not working. Fix the delivery; content changes go through section 07.

## Week 6: Build the target list and start outreach

The outreach lead builds a list of 30 organizations using section 04: ten senior centers, ten libraries, ten faith or community organizations, each with a named contact, phone, and email. This takes about four hours and is the most important four hours in this plan.

Send the first ten emails on a Tuesday using the templates in section 04. Log each in the tracker. Now you may make a flyer and a social account (section 09); you have something to announce.

## Week 7: Follow up and call

Day-5 follow-ups go out for week 6's emails. Day-10 calls begin. Send the second batch of ten emails. You will probably book your first talk this week, and it will probably be a library, because libraries book quickly and have calendars to fill.

## Week 8: Book your first three talks

By the end of week 8 you want three talks on the calendar within six weeks. If you have one, keep calling. If you have zero, it is almost always one of three things: emails are going to a generic inbox instead of a named activities director, you are not calling, or you are only emailing campus-adjacent organizations. Section 04 fixes all three.

Send Every Shade (Rutgers) a two-line update: organizations contacted, talks booked. That is your first report.

## After week 8

A healthy chapter gives two to four talks a month during the semester, runs the quiz at every one, sends the 30-day follow-up, and reports twice a year. A chapter that gives one talk a semester is still one room of people who now check their feet. Do not grow faster than your speakers can handle.
